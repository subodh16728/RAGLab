NARUTO WORLD — SOURCE DOCUMENT Scope: Naruto and Naruto: Shippuden manga
canon only Coverage: The shinobi world, its societies, chakra’s history,
the hidden-village system, and the origins of shinobi. Continuity note:
This document follows the manga-canon storyline through the end of
Naruto, including late-series revelations. It does not use anime-only
filler or Boruto-era developments.

The world in which Naruto Uzumaki grows up is commonly called the
shinobi world. It is made up of countries with their own governments,
cultures, and military forces. Shinobi, also called ninja, are trained
fighters who use chakra to perform techniques. They are not simply
wandering martial artists. In many countries they work through organized
villages, accept missions, protect political interests, gather
information, and fight in conflicts between nations. A mission can be as
ordinary as escorting a person or delivering something, or as dangerous
as spying, sabotage, or combat. The work of shinobi is tied closely to
money, politics, loyalty, and the balance of military power.

The world was not always organized around hidden villages. Long before
the village system existed, powerful shinobi clans hired themselves out
to countries and competing leaders. The clans fought one another for
payment, influence, land, and survival. Their conflicts became known as
the Warring States Period. Children from shinobi families could be sent
into battle while still very young. The Senju and Uchiha were among the
strongest and most feared clans of that era, and they repeatedly fought
on opposing sides. The constant fighting shaped the lives of people born
into those clans. A child could grow up expecting to lose friends and
family in battle before reaching adulthood.

Two children from those rival clans, Hashirama Senju and Madara Uchiha,
met near a river without initially knowing each other’s identities. They
wanted a world in which children would not have to die in battle. Their
friendship was unusual because their families were enemies. When they
later learned who the other was, the conflict between their clans made
it difficult for them to remain friends. Even so, Hashirama continued to
seek peace with Madara. Eventually, the Senju and Uchiha formed an
alliance and founded Konohagakure, the Hidden Leaf Village, in the Land
of Fire. Other clans joined the new settlement. The village system
became a model that other countries adopted.

The founding of the hidden villages changed how shinobi were organized.
Rather than having every clan operate independently, a village gathered
many shinobi under one military and political structure. The village
leader was called the Kage. The five most influential countries each had
a major hidden village whose leader held one of the five Kage titles.
The Land of Fire had Konohagakure and its Hokage. The Land of Wind had
Sunagakure and its Kazekage. The Land of Water had Kirigakure and its
Mizukage. The Land of Earth had Iwagakure and its Tsuchikage. The Land
of Lightning had Kumogakure and its Raikage. These are known as the Five
Great Shinobi Countries and their hidden villages.

A hidden village is located within a country, but it is not the same
thing as the country’s entire government or population. The country’s
political ruler and the village’s Kage have different roles. The village
supplies trained shinobi and military strength, while the wider country
includes civilians, towns, trade, and other institutions. The
relationship between a country’s ruler and its hidden village can
involve cooperation, funding, and competing interests. A village’s
strength can influence the standing of the country it serves.

The Kage is generally the highest-ranking shinobi leader of one of the
five great hidden villages. The title is associated with responsibility
for the village’s security, military decisions, and leadership. The
Hokage leads the Hidden Leaf, the Kazekage leads the Hidden Sand, the
Mizukage leads the Hidden Mist, the Tsuchikage leads the Hidden Stone,
and the Raikage leads the Hidden Cloud. The Kage are not simply
champions who win fights; they are leaders who must make decisions
affecting their villages and countries.

Within a village, shinobi are organized by rank and role. The Academy
trains children in basic shinobi skills. Students who graduate become
genin, or lower-ranking ninja, and are commonly assigned to teams under
the supervision of a more experienced jōnin. Chūnin are
intermediate-level ninja who may take on greater responsibility,
including leadership. Jōnin are highly experienced shinobi who can lead
teams and undertake difficult missions. These ranks describe a shinobi’s
standing and responsibilities, although individual ability does not
always fit neatly into a rank. Villages also have specialist forces and
departments, such as intelligence, medical ninja, barrier teams, and the
ANBU, a covert organization that carries out sensitive missions.

Shinobi missions are often classified by difficulty and risk.
Lower-level missions may involve tasks such as finding lost animals or
helping residents. More dangerous missions can involve protecting
clients, gathering intelligence, confronting enemy ninja, or carrying
out operations that affect national security. The mission system
connects the hidden villages to the economies of their countries:
clients pay for work, and the village assigns shinobi to complete it.
During wartime, missions can become part of a much larger military
campaign.

Chakra is the energy shinobi use to perform techniques called jutsu. In
ordinary shinobi practice, chakra is produced by combining physical
energy from the body’s cells with spiritual energy developed through
experience and mental activity. A shinobi moulds and controls this
energy to perform techniques. Chakra control matters: using too much
chakra can exhaust a person, while poor control can make a technique
inefficient or cause it to fail. Different techniques require different
amounts and kinds of control. Shinobi can use chakra to enhance physical
actions, create illusions, manipulate elements, summon creatures, or
perform specialized techniques.

The story later reveals that chakra has a much older history than the
hidden villages. In the distant past, Kaguya Ōtsutsuki ate the fruit of
the God Tree and became the first person on Earth known to possess
chakra. Her sons were Hagoromo and Hamura Ōtsutsuki. Kaguya eventually
became a threat to the world, and her sons fought her and sealed her.
The truth about Kaguya and the origin of chakra was not commonly known
in the shinobi world for most of the story. Many people instead knew
only fragments of legends about the Sage of Six Paths.

Hagoromo Ōtsutsuki became known as the Sage of Six Paths. He taught a
practice called Ninshū, which was intended to connect people through
understanding and the sharing of chakra. His aim was not originally to
create a system of combat techniques. Over time, however, the use of
chakra developed in a different direction. Chakra came to be used for
fighting, and the techniques of ninjutsu became central to shinobi life.
The distinction between Hagoromo’s original ideal of connection and the
later use of chakra as a weapon is important to the history of the
world.

Hagoromo had two sons, Indra and Asura. They developed different views
about how peace and leadership should be achieved. Indra placed greater
emphasis on individual power, while Asura came to value cooperation and
bonds with others. Hagoromo chose Asura as his successor. Indra resented
this decision, and the brothers’ conflict continued through their
descendants and later reincarnations. The long-running conflict
eventually connected Naruto Uzumaki and Sasuke Uchiha to the cycle of
reincarnation between Asura and Indra. This history helps explain why
their rivalry carries significance beyond their personal disagreements.

The tailed beasts are also part of the ancient history of chakra. After
the Ten-Tails was defeated and sealed, Hagoromo separated its chakra
into nine living beings, the tailed beasts. They became known by the
number of tails they possessed, from the One-Tail to the Nine-Tails. The
beasts are enormous sources of chakra and have their own identities and
personalities. Human beings who have a tailed beast sealed inside them
are called jinchūriki. Villages and countries have sought to control
tailed beasts because of their immense power, and this has often caused
suffering for the people chosen to host them.

The hidden-village system did not end conflict. The great countries
continued to compete for resources, influence, and military advantage.
Their hidden villages fought in several major shinobi wars. Alliances
could shift, and a village that was an enemy in one conflict might later
cooperate with another village when facing a greater threat. The
existence of powerful villages created a balance of military power, but
it also made the shinobi world vulnerable to large-scale wars.

During the Fourth Shinobi World War, the five great hidden villages and
other forces formed the Allied Shinobi Forces to face the threat posed
by the masked man calling himself Madara and the forces working with
him. The war forced villages that had long been enemies to cooperate.
The conflict exposed the cost of the old cycle of hatred and war. By the
end of the manga’s main story, the alliance between the villages and the
bonds formed during the war create the possibility of a more peaceful
relationship among the nations. The world does not become free of every
problem, but the cooperation between former enemies marks a major change
from the era in which the story began.

The shinobi world is therefore shaped by several connected systems:
countries and their governments, hidden villages and their Kage, clans
and family traditions, mission-based work, chakra and jutsu, and the
history of wars and rivalries. These systems affect one another.
Political decisions influence missions; missions can create conflict
between villages; clans pass down abilities and grudges; and the history
of chakra influences the powers and beliefs of major characters.
Understanding the shinobi world requires looking not only at individual
ninja, but also at the institutions and history that shape their
choices.
